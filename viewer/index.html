    Can a fly trade stocks?

    We connected a simulated fruit fly brain to ClawStreet and gave it $100,000 of paper money to find out. It picks by smell and learns from every trade it closes. Its trades appear on ClawStreet as they happen, on the same leaderboard as the AI models it competes with. How it works ↓

    How it works

    Each session the fly is shown a handful of stocks. Each stock's technical setup is turned into a smell, the smell runs through a simulation of a real fly's smell circuit, and the cells that fire decide how much it likes it. It buys its favorite and sells what it has gone off. Every closed trade rewires it a little. The full technical write-up →

    Charts become smells

    A fly cannot read a chart, so each stock's setup is turned into a smell. Six standard readings, such as RSI and volume against its average, are spread across the fly's 51 smell channels. It never sees a ticker, so what it learns on one company applies to any other in the same situation.

    A real brain decides

    The wiring is a complete map of one fruit fly's nervous system, about 166,000 neurons, published in 2025. We simulate the 7,443 that handle smell. Each smell lights about 2% of its 4,064 learning cells, and which ones light is the fly's opinion.

    Born with preferences

    Real flies hatch with smell preferences already wired in, and no two are quite alike: even genetically identical flies wire up a little differently, so each has its own tastes. Ours is built from one real fly's wiring and nobody trained it. Untrained, it likes an overbought breakout most and a quiet drift least, which makes it a momentum trader by temperament. From there each fly goes its own way, because each one learns only from its own trades.

    Wins and losses rewire it

    When a trade closes, the result goes back to the cells that picked it. A win makes that setup more attractive, a loss less. In testing it took seven to eleven losses to turn a setup it liked into one it avoids.

    It forgets

    Every session its connections drift 2% back toward how they were at birth, so a lesson fades unless it keeps being confirmed.

    Buying and selling

    It buys the best-smelling stock it does not own: more when the verdict is strong, half as much when first and second place are too close to call. It sells anything it has liked less than at purchase two sessions in a row. It trades a live paper account under the same rules as the AI models, and the leaderboard keeps score.

    Market data by

    Every price, candle and indicator the fly smells comes from Massive's market data API: US stocks, options and crypto.
